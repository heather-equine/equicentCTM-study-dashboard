# Equicenta CTM Sales Dashboard Implementation Brief (Review Draft)

This draft brief defines the proposed content and UX updates for the existing Equicenta CTM sales dashboard before any live deployment. It is based on the published clinical publication kit, the messaging guide, the master statements list, and the one-page sell-sheet structure in the attached dossier.[cite:1]

## Review assumptions

This draft is intended for pre-live review only and assumes the existing dashboard shell remains in place while content, modules, labels, and compliance behavior are revised.[cite:18] The publication kit requires that external-facing claims map to source-backed statements, respect evidence tiering, and follow the messaging guardrails for audience, qualifiers, and prohibited phrasing.[cite:1]

A client-directed legal copy override applies to the footer language in this review draft. The source kit includes investigational disclaimer language beginning with “Important information: equicenta® CTM is under investigation,” but that opening sentence is intentionally removed from this review version pending use of the company’s updated approved footer language.[cite:1]

## Global content rules

All numeric and comparative claims used in the dashboard should map to the master marketing statements list in the publication kit, which contains 37 statements categorized by claim type, paper source, and evidentiary tier.[cite:1] Primary claims are the default foundation for external-facing dashboard content, while Secondary, Exploratory, and Supporting claims should be clearly labeled and only used in the appropriate modules.[cite:1]

Exploratory claims must visibly carry qualifier language such as “in an exploratory analysis” or “in a sub-class analysis.”[cite:1] Supporting claims must not be presented as direct findings of the trial, and product-brand-specific comparisons, curative wording, and “no side effects” language are prohibited in the source kit’s guardrails.[cite:1]

## Navigation plan

The recommended structure keeps the current dashboard intact and adds publication-driven modules in a web-app pattern that supports fast scanning, persistent navigation, and drill-down detail panels.[cite:18] The proposed tab set is: Overview, Clinical Study, Performance, Safety, Imaging & Motion, Competitive Positioning, Sales Sheets, Internal Enablement, and Compliance Review.[cite:1]

| Tab | Purpose | Content basis |
|---|---|---|
| Overview | Fast summary of the publication story | Study rigor, efficacy, performance, safety, differentiation.[cite:1] |
| Clinical Study | Scientific proof and study structure | Study design, follow-up, efficacy, onset.[cite:1] |
| Performance | Return-to-work and owner relevance | Performance, return-to-work, trainer guide, owner-reported outcomes.[cite:1] |
| Safety | Risk and injection confidence | Safety, injection-site, reaction profile.[cite:1] |
| Imaging & Motion | Mechanistic and objective support | Structural healing, Sleip, forelimb symmetry.[cite:1] |
| Competitive Positioning | Class-level commercial differentiation | Hemoderivatives, APS subgroup, durability subgroup, product differentiation.[cite:1] |
| Sales Sheets | Interactive field-asset library | 19 customer-facing one-pagers.[cite:1] |
| Internal Enablement | Restricted internal sales support | Economics, logistics, objection handling.[cite:1][cite:26] |
| Compliance Review | Claim controls and usage rules | Evidence tiers, qualifiers, footer logic, say/don’t-say guidance.[cite:1] |

## Overview tab

The Overview tab should serve as the quickest credible summary of the publication for live field use.[cite:1] It should present a short hero statement, four KPI cards, and clear navigation into the Clinical Study and Sales Sheets areas.[cite:1]

### Proposed copy

**Headline:** “Built on evidence. Designed for scrutiny.”[cite:1]

**Subhead:** “Peer-reviewed, controlled clinical study in performance horses showing earlier symptom improvement, stronger return-to-work outcomes, and a cleaner reaction profile versus clinician-selected standard of care.”[cite:1]

**KPI cards:**
- “138 performance horses · 195 conditions · 8 U.S. practices.”[cite:1]
- “100% completed 12-week primary endpoint.”[cite:1]
- “Pain and swelling separated by week 2; lameness by week 4.”[cite:1]
- “79% vs 21% returned to or exceeded prior performance at 26 weeks.”[cite:1]

**Buttons:**
- “Open Clinical Study.”
- “Browse Sales Sheets.”
- Optional utility action: “Copy Approved Claim.”[cite:1]

## Clinical Study tab

The Clinical Study tab should be the scientific anchor of the dashboard and should prioritize rigor before promotional framing, especially for veterinary audiences.[cite:1] It should be divided into study design, follow-up completeness, primary efficacy, onset of action, and a source footer block.[cite:1]

### Module 1: Study design and rigor

Recommended headline: “Study design you can defend.”[cite:1]

Recommended bullet content:
- “Largest controlled clinical trial of its kind in performance horses.”[cite:1]
- “Prospective multicenter controlled trial, CONSORT-guided.”[cite:1]
- “138 horses, 195 conditions, 8 practices.”[cite:1]
- “Quasi-randomized within practice at a 1.9:1 achieved allocation ratio.”[cite:1]
- “Independent third-party biostatistics.”[cite:1]

The source kit explicitly says not to label the study as a randomized controlled trial; the dashboard should use “prospective multicenter controlled trial” and “quasi-randomized within practice” instead.[cite:1]

### Module 2: Follow-up completeness

Recommended headline: “No horses lost at the primary endpoint.”[cite:1]

Recommended content:
- “100% follow-up at 12 weeks (138/138 horses).”[cite:1]
- “98% follow-up at 26 weeks (135/138 horses).”[cite:1]
- “Every enrolled horse was assessed at the pre-specified primary endpoint; no imputation or missing-data adjustment was required.”[cite:1]

### Module 3: Primary efficacy and onset

Recommended headline: “Earlier, greater, and sustained improvement.”[cite:1]

Recommended content:
- “Greater cumulative improvement than clinician-selected standard of care in lameness, pain, and swelling through 12 weeks (p<0.001 all outcomes).”[cite:1]
- “Outperformed standard of care on pain and swelling by 2 weeks and lameness by 4 weeks.”[cite:1]
- “3:1 higher odds of being pain-free at 12 weeks (p<0.001).”[cite:1]

## Performance tab

The Performance tab should make the 26-week return-to-work story easy to communicate to owners, trainers, and referral-oriented sport-horse veterinarians.[cite:1] The messaging guide identifies this as the most quotable owner-facing narrative in the publication set.[cite:1]

### Proposed copy

**Headline:** “The endpoint owners actually care about — returning to work.”[cite:1]

**Primary metrics:**
- “79% of eCTM horses returned to or exceeded prior performance at 26 weeks vs 21% standard of care.”[cite:1]
- “2.72:1 higher odds of returning to or exceeding prior performance (p<0.006).”[cite:1]
- “Large-magnitude performance advantage at 26 weeks (Cohen’s d = 2.23; p<0.001).”[cite:1]

**Owner-friendly panel:**
- “4 out of 5 horses on eCTM returned to or exceeded their prior level of work at 6 months.”[cite:1]

**Support panel:**
- “Advantage preserved in both intent-to-treat and per-protocol analyses.”[cite:1]
- “Owner-reported outcomes also favored eCTM across body condition, hair coat, overall health, and quality of life at 26 weeks.”[cite:1]

The source kit prohibits promise language such as “guaranteed return to work,” and the dashboard should not extrapolate the results to disciplines or use-cases not represented in the study.[cite:1]

## Safety tab

The Safety tab should become one of the strongest scientific-commercial modules because the messaging guide recommends leading with safety before efficacy for joint-injection-focused audiences.[cite:1] It should separate overall reaction results from injection-site results, while keeping the framing conservative and publication-bound.[cite:1]

### Proposed copy

**Headline:** “A cleaner reaction profile than the products it will replace.”[cite:1]

**Primary metrics:**
- “Significantly fewer reported adverse reactions than standard of care.”[cite:1]
- “4.8:1 lower reported reaction ratio in vet-reported analysis (p<0.04).”[cite:1]
- “5.9:1 lower ratio under conservative central review (p=0.002).”[cite:1]
- “3.4% per-injection reaction rate in the eCTM arm (5/148).”[cite:1]
- “Zero infections and zero vet-reported product-related serious adverse events in the eCTM arm.”[cite:1]

**Injection-site panel:**
- “Injection-site inflammation was a prospectively defined endpoint using a validated 0–4 scale.”[cite:1]
- “0/4 injection-site inflammation score at every follow-up for 100% of eCTM injections.”[cite:1]
- “Reported reactions were mild, self-limited, and appeared early after injection.”[cite:1]

The source kit explicitly prohibits “zero side effects” wording, so the dashboard should use “fewer reported adverse reactions” and related source-backed language only.[cite:1]

## Imaging & Motion tab

This tab should provide deeper mechanistic support for skeptical clinicians and should visually distinguish exploratory evidence from primary findings.[cite:1] Structural healing and kinematic findings are categorized as Exploratory in the master statements list and therefore require visible qualifier language in the UI.[cite:1]

### Structural healing module

Recommended section label: “Exploratory analysis.”[cite:1]

Recommended content:
- “In an exploratory analysis, 36% of eCTM-treated ligaments reached ‘Responding’ on Tissue Remodeling Score by 12 weeks vs 0% of standard of care.”[cite:1]
- “Largest structural effect was observed in proximal suspensory ligament lesions.”[cite:1]

### Kinematic module

Recommended content:
- “Objective sensor-based gait analysis (Sleip) confirmed the clinical findings, with greater forelimb symmetry improvement in eCTM-treated horses.”[cite:1]
- “Push-off improvement was significant at week 2 and week 12; impact improvement at week 4.”[cite:1]

The dashboard should never imply that Sleip diagnosed cases; the source guide says the sensor measured improvement but did not diagnose disease.[cite:1]

## Competitive Positioning tab

This tab should frame class-level replacement and differentiation without crossing into prohibited brand-specific comparison language.[cite:1] The publication kit allows class-level hemoderivative comparison, APS subgroup context, and off-the-shelf differentiation when properly qualified.[cite:1]

### Proposed modules

**Comparator context:**
- “Standard of care reflected the real-world biologics spectrum used by experienced clinicians, including APS, PRP, ACS, PPP, MSCs, amnion, and HA.”[cite:1]
- “Hemoderivatives made up 64% of the standard-of-care arm.”[cite:1]

**Head-to-head module (Exploratory):**
- “In a pre-specified head-to-head contrast, eCTM outperformed hemoderivatives on lameness and pain.”[cite:1]
- “APS represented 51% of the hemoderivative subgroup and showed the same directional advantage.”[cite:1]

**Differentiation module:**
- “One thaw, one injection, consistent lot-to-lot composition.”[cite:1]
- “One 1.5 mL intra-articular injection versus standard of care often requiring 2–3 injections.”[cite:1]
- “Off-the-shelf cryopreserved umbilical allograft with no chairside blood processing.”[cite:1]

The source guide prohibits “better than [Trade Name]” and also prohibits calling the product stem cell therapy.[cite:1]

## Sales Sheets section

The new Sales Sheets section should operate as an interactive evidence library rather than a PDF dump.[cite:1] The source kit includes 19 customer-facing one-pagers covering study rigor, follow-up, efficacy, onset, performance, safety, structural healing, Sleip, symmetry, comparator content, differentiation, case selection, and a launch playbook.[cite:1]

### Recommended filters

- Audience: Vet, Owner/Trainer, Distributor/Sales.[cite:1]
- Claim type: Rigor, Efficacy, Performance, Safety, Imaging, Comparator, Differentiation, Practice Adoption.[cite:1]
- Evidence tier: Primary, Secondary, Exploratory, Supporting.[cite:1]
- Use case: First call, objection response, leave-behind, follow-up, launch support.[cite:1]

### Card template

Each sell-sheet card should include:
- Title.[cite:1]
- Audience tags.[cite:1]
- Headline claim.[cite:1]
- One key stat.[cite:1]
- Evidence tier badge.[cite:1]
- Actions: “Preview Summary,” “Copy Approved Language,” and “Open Full Sheet.”[cite:1]

## Internal Enablement section

The publication kit marks three assets as internal-only: Economics of the Switch, Cold-chain/Logistics & Inventory, and Objection Handler.[cite:1] Those assets should not appear in public or rep-facing views and should instead follow the same admin-only visibility pattern previously used for restricted dashboard content.[cite:25][cite:26]

Recommended items:
- “Economics of the switch.”[cite:1]
- “Cold-chain, logistics & inventory.”[cite:1]
- “Objection handler: 10 hardest questions.”[cite:1]

## Compliance Review section

A dedicated compliance layer should make claim status, qualifier requirements, and approved phrasing transparent before any content reaches the live dashboard.[cite:1] This section should act as a review tool for internal users and as a logic source for badges and footers across the rest of the dashboard.[cite:1]

Recommended components:
- Tier badge dictionary: Primary, Secondary, Exploratory, Supporting, Primary + Literature, Primary (Sensitivity), Primary (Near-threshold). [cite:1]
- Qualifier banner for exploratory modules: “Use ‘in an exploratory analysis’ or equivalent language.”[cite:1]
- Say / Don’t Say snippets pulled from the category playbook.[cite:1]
- Footer language component with client-approved override support.[cite:1]

## Review footer language

The following footer format is recommended for this review draft in place of the source kit’s current “under investigation” opening sentence.[cite:1]

“Results summarized here are from a single prospective controlled clinical study in performance horses; individual results may vary. Source: Bertone AL, Davern A, Sutter WW, et al. Front. Vet. Sci. 2026. DOI 10.3389/fvets.2026.1833933. IACUC ETCR-2024-0329 · AVMA VCT #26005934.”[cite:1]

## Build sequence

The recommended implementation order is: Overview, Clinical Study, Safety, Performance, Imaging & Motion, Competitive Positioning, Sales Sheets, Internal Enablement, then global compliance components.[cite:1] This sequence aligns with the user’s broader dashboard preference for controlled live evolution within a single source-of-truth system rather than disruptive rebuilds or parallel versions.[cite:21]
